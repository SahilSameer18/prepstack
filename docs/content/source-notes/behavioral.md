# Behavioral guide source notes (11 Nov 2026)

Facts about how to answer are restated in our own words. The questions, STAR outlines and example answers are written by us for students; the example answers are made-up examples (no real company, person or number is claimed) and the page labels them "use it as a guide, not a script". Status: read by us; two AI reviewers (not an outside reader).

## Pages read (11 Nov 2026)
- MIT CAPD, "Using the STAR method for your next behavioral interview" (loaded, article read): STAR = Situation, Task, Action, Result. Suggested split about 20% situation, 10% task, 60% action, 10% result. Interviewers want real behaviours, a clear personal role ("I" not "we"), specific examples tied to the job. Mistakes: bending the truth, "we" answers that hide your role, answers that are too general, memorised scripts, not linking the story to the job. No total length given; no licence line.
- Wikipedia "Situation, task, action, result" (CC BY-SA 4.0): the four letters; Task may be replaced by Target in some methods; areas: conflict, time management, problem solving, interpersonal skills. The "Behavioral interview" page is a 404; "Job interview" read in part (first 40k of 289k characters, rest by summary): behavioral questions ask about past handling of job-relevant situations, past behaviour predicts future behaviour.
- Harvard FAS Mignone Center, "Interviewing" guide (loaded): behaviour themes: working with different people, being highly motivated, handling differing opinions, working under pressure, a difficult co-worker, creative problem solving, leadership, a mistake or failure. Mistakes flagged: being vague, rambling, no concluding outcome or lesson, blaming others, generic personality traits instead of behaviours. This page uses Situation, Action, Result (no Task).
- Indeed, "10 Behavioral Interview Questions (With Sample Answers)", updated June 2026 (loaded): STAR; themes: team conflict, admitting a mistake, deadlines, failure and learning, going beyond your role, leading or motivating, a task you were not trained for, a goal you achieved, a hard decision, your problem-solving process; tips: be honest, review your accomplishments, keep answers to about 1 to 2 minutes. Terms: informational only; no licence.
- GeeksforGeeks "Behavioral Interview Question and Answers" (3 Aug 2026): themes: challenges, pressure, mistakes, goals, disagreement, team vs solo work, motivating a teammate. Quality notes: sample answers are generic with no specifics, several invite memorising, its extra list leans toward customer-facing roles. Used only to check which themes are asked.
- InterviewBit HR interview questions (first ~40k of 90k characters verbatim, rest by summary): STAR spelled out; checks: honesty and ownership, self-awareness, taking criticism, culture fit, pressure; mistakes verbatim: don't bluff, don't disparage past employers, avoid cliché answers, cap weaknesses at two. Factual slips found in its non-technical questions (lion "king of the jungle", an unsourced salary figure) are not used.
- Google re:Work, "A guide to structured interviewing for better hiring practices" (updated Mar 2026): behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time...") test past experience; hypothetical questions test reasoning on new problems; scoring uses a rubric; interviewers look for reasoning not one right answer. No licence line.
- Amazon "Leadership Principles" (16 principles, including Ownership, Bias for Action, Learn and Be Curious, Dive Deep, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Deliver Results, Earn Trust): used only to name themes (ownership, going deeper to the cause, committing after disagreement, learning). No text copied. Copyright Amazon.

## Wording and design choices (ours)
- 10 groups of 5 questions: Personal, Teamwork, Conflict, Failure and learning, Leadership, Ownership, Deadlines and pressure, Ambiguity and learning, Feedback, Motivation. The old "Problem Solving" group was split across Ownership and Ambiguity and learning; ids 1 to 22 stay, ids 23 to 50 are new.
- Each question: what the interviewer is looking for (our one-sentence reading of the pages above), four STAR hints, an example answer of two to four sentences, and two mistakes. Written in easy words for a reader with no technical background.
