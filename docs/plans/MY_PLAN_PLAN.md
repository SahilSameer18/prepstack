## Product principles (owner, 13 Nov 2026)
The aim is that **anyone can follow PrepStack and get ready for a job like the big-tech ones**. No roadmap can guarantee a job, and PrepStack never says it can; it can be as accurate and as easy as possible. So: (1) **accurate**: every number and rule is sourced, computed by script or run, and reviewed before it is shown, and the plan says what it left out; (2) **very easy to use and understand**: few fields, plain words, one obvious next step on every screen; (3) **honest**: estimates are shown as estimates, no promise of results; (4) **free, with an account**: PrepStack is free and there is no payment anywhere in it; the gate is the login: anyone may open the form and see a preview, but generating, saving and tracking a plan need an account.

## What My plan is
**The planner no other prep site has: one plan for everything a student must do, built from the time they really have.** One daily checklist for one goal, built by code (not AI), shown as a **My plan** tab on the dashboard; the form and its preview are open to everyone, and generating the plan needs login. A student says what they are aiming for and what hours they have each day of the week; the plan fits the right DSA topics, the four CS notes, a roadmap and behavioral questions into their days, plans to 85 percent of their hours, tells them honestly what fits, tracks the time they really spend against the plan, reviews each week, and rebuilds the rest with a preview when they fall behind. A Role Analyzer report can add its tasks to the same plan. The owner uses it for his own big-tech SDE preparation from December 2026.

**Why it can be the differentiator (from the research).** Every product I used is DSA-only; only Grind 75 turns time into a plan and only by weeks; Planly (takeUforward) does days and tracks time but puts rebuild behind a pause, limits free users to one undeletable plan and six hours a day, and cannot mix subjects. My plan: all subjects in one plan, a plan from a job description (Role Analyzer), a preview before you sign up, honest coverage numbers, a preview before any change, real time tracking, a weekly review that links weak topics to our notes and quizzes, and a free delete.

## Decisions (all owner decided)
**First round, 13 Nov 2026** (5 to 8 were my recommendations, agreed the same day)
1. **Build now, check on v1.** The student check (3 to 5 students, 2 to 3 job switchers) is run on the working version; the answers decide what to cut or change, not whether to build.
2. **One tick for DSA problems.** Ticking a DSA problem in My plan marks it solved on its sheet (counts for the streak); a problem already solved on the sheet shows done in the plan.
3. **Goal types, three:** "Placements in N weeks", "Switch to a role in N weeks", "Big-tech SDE interview" (a DSA sheet, the four CS notes, and the HLD and LLD notes once they exist).
4. **Hours per weekday and per weekend day, plus a chosen rest day.**
5. **The generator runs on the server.** `server/src/data/roadmapCatalog.json` is written by a script from `client/src/data/roadmaps.js`; a test fails when the two differ.
6. **Quiz time:** every notes item gets 5 minutes for the quiz (the rest of its estimate is changed by decision 29).
7. **When there is nothing today:** "Today" shows the next day that has items, labelled with its date.
8. **After the deadline:** the plan stays readable, shows "finished" with the totals, and offers "Start a new plan".

**Second round, 13 Nov 2026 (the owner took my recommendations from the competitor research; the public preview was removed and then restored in the form described in decision 9)**
9. **The form and its preview are open; generating needs login.** Anyone can open the My plan page, fill the form and see the preview without an account. **A logged-out preview shows the numbers (total hours, finish date, coverage, what was left out) and week 1 only; the other weeks appear after login.** Generating (saving) the plan, ticking, the review and the revision list need login; after login the settings carry through and the plan is created from them. The settings live in the address, so a plan can be shared as a link, and the page is public so search engines can read it. (In takeUforward, when I was logged out, "Plan Now" went straight to a login dialog with no preview; ours shows a preview first and asks for login only to generate.)
10. **Core first when time is short.** Each DSA topic is labelled **core** or **extra** by us (core = the topic exists in the Blind 75 sheet; CS notes and roadmap steps are all core). When the content does not fit, extra topics go first, then the tail of the core; if even the core does not fit, the plan says so and suggests the Blind 75 list. No frequency claims are made.
11. **Plan to 85 percent of the stated hours** (a fixed rule, shown plainly: "planned for 85 percent of your hours so one bad day does not break the plan"), because the Tech Interview Handbook advises conservative estimates. **The 85 percent is split: up to 70 percent of the hours for new items and up to 15 percent for due reviews (decision 37); the last 15 percent is free time.**
12. **Order by dependency.** DSA topics follow the order of our DSA roadmap (arrays, two pointers, and so on), through a mapping file `server/src/data/planCatalog.json` with each sheet topic's order and core or extra label; a test fails when a topic of a sheet has no entry.
13. **Study style, one question with a default:** "One topic at a time, then mix" (default) or "Mix from the start".
14. **No time tracking in version 1** (owner decision, 13 Nov 2026; this replaces the earlier "real time tracking"). There is no timer, no time spent and no start view with a clock. Every item shows its **planned** minutes (an estimate) and the student ticks it when done. Reasons: a timer is not proof of effort, it adds a second thing to manage on a screen that should be one calm list, and it needs its own anti-cheating and privacy code; everything the plan needs (on track, reviews, the weekly review) works from planned minutes and done items. A mock block shows a 90-minute countdown on the screen and saves nothing. It can come back after the student check if students ask for it.
15. **"On track" status.** A line on the plan, "On track" or "2 days behind", next to the rebuild button.
16. **A preview before any change.** Rebuild from today and changing the hours (allowed at any time, not only while paused) first show the result: the new finish date ("stays the same" or "N days earlier or later"), how many items move and that **done work is protected**; then a confirm.
17. **A weekly review** (computed when opened, not stored): planned minutes against the minutes of the items that were done, items done and moved, the weak topics linked to the notes sections and their 5-question quizzes, and an offer to rebuild.
18. **A revision list.** A star on any item puts it on a list; starred items are reviewed first (see decisions 21 and 22).
19. **A delete that always works,** and a deleted plan is really gone; deleting the account removes plans (Planly's paywalled delete is the thing not to copy). Nothing in PrepStack is paid.
**Third round, 13 Nov 2026 (the owner took all nine proposals R1 to R9 from the second research round)**
20. **A three-button result when an item is ticked:** "Solved alone", "With hints", "Needed the solution" (notes sections use the quiz score instead). One tap, never typing. It drives the review ladder and the weekly review's weak topics.
21. **A short review ladder driven by that result.** Alone: reviews at day 7 and day 30, then done. With hints: day 3, 14 and 45. Needed the solution: day 1, 4, 14 and 45. A passed review moves to the next gap; a failed review goes back to the first gap. A review is a 2 to 3 minute recall (name the pattern, the key idea, the complexity, one edge case); a full re-code is offered only after a failed review. CS notes sections are reviewed by **retaking their 5-question quiz** (4 of 5 or more passes) at day 3, 10 and 30. The gaps are our design from sources that agree on growing gaps (the research round 2 note), not a measured result.
22. **A daily review cap and a backlog rule.** Due reviews use at most **15 percent of a day's hours**, failed and starred first, then oldest; what does not fit waits and is never deleted; **no new item is added while the overdue reviews are more than one day's cap.** This replaces the plain weekly revision block (the weekly review still shows what was reviewed).
23. **Skip and Move are separate actions.** Skip keeps the topic on a visible "skipped" list and warns when later topics build on it (our dependency order); Move keeps the item with a new date, at most 3 days from where it was and never onto a mock or checkpoint day.
24. **Never silently remove or move.** Yesterday's missed items show at the top of today as "carried over" (a view only; the plan's dates change only when the student rebuilds). At **2 or more days behind, or 3 or more missed items**, the plan suggests a rebuild (a suggestion with a button, never an automatic change).
25. **Lighter days.** The student can set 1 to 14 days at fewer hours (exams, travel, illness) with a return to normal afterwards, through the same preview and confirm.
26. **Manual moves are locked.** An item the student places by hand stays there when the plan is rebuilt.
27. **Start small.** The Start button opens the smallest item of the day first; "just open it and read for 5 minutes" counts as starting.
28. **Overflow order.** When time is short: dated items first (a behavioral week, mock blocks), then core, then extra; anything left out is listed as "could not fit" with a link to add it.
**Fourth round, 13 Nov 2026 (the owner: "whatever is good for PrepStack and improve my plan"; my recommendations from the worked example and a review of the plan, applied)**
29. **Realistic estimates.** A DSA problem counts the time to solve it **and to check the solution**: Basic 30, Easy 40, Medium 70, Hard 120 minutes (Grind 75's page shows 64 hours where its listed question times add up to about 32, because it assumes about the same time again to check the answer). A notes section counts 3 times its computed reading time plus 5 minutes for the quiz (our judgement: reading time is only reading). Everything stays labelled as an estimate and editable; after enough timer data (version 1.1) the plan offers "use my own average".
30. **No gaps in a day.** When the next item does not fit in the rest of a day, the generator takes a smaller item of the same topic that does fit, or splits the big item into parts, so days fill close to their 85 percent.
31. **Spare time is used, not wasted.** When the content takes less time than the deadline allows, the plan adds a second pass (reviews, starred and unsolved items), mock blocks, behavioral practice and the HLD and LLD notes once published, and says how many weeks early it finishes.
32. **Already solved or known is not scheduled.** A problem already solved on the sheet is shown done and takes no time; the form has an optional step "topics you already know" that leaves those topics out (they sit on the skipped list as "already know"); a problem that appears in two topics (the same link) is scheduled once and ticking it ticks both.
33. **One calm Today screen, kept sacred** (see "The first screen" below; owner rule, 13 Nov 2026: this decision is golden). When a student opens the dashboard they see only what they need to do today, the time needed, and one big Start button. Graphs, the review overview, the weekly review, settings and every complex menu live inside the tabs. **Nothing may be added to the Today screen without the owner saying so**, and a client test fails if it shows anything beyond the allowed parts.
34. **Plan states.** A plan is `active` or `finished`; a finished plan stays readable as history and a new plan can be started; the unique index is one active plan per user.
35. **An acceptance example as a test.** The worked example (big-tech SDE interview, 12 weeks, Monday to Friday 3 hours, Saturday 5, Sunday rest, start Friday 13 Nov 2026, NeetCode 150 and the four CS notes) is a fixed test on the real data with the corrected estimates: its syllabus hours, weekly hours (17), coverage and finish date are asserted, and so are the 8-week and 6-week versions (core first, then the tail). A rule change that moves them fails a test. The numbers are computed by running the generator and checked by hand once. Also tested: the day budget (70 percent new, up to 15 percent reviews), a mock block (90 minutes, two unsolved core problems, Saturday from week 4), the ref kinds and merge minutes (decision 39), review items surviving a rebuild.
36. **Data fix in Phase 0.** The NeetCode 150 seed has 151 entries: **Subsets II is missing** (and "N Queens" should read "N-Queens"), **Word Search II appears in both Backtracking and Trie** and **Longest Common Subsequence in both 1-D and 2-D dynamic programming** (checked on 13 Nov against neetcode.io, which lists 150: Easy 28, Medium 101, Hard 21; Backtracking 10, 1-D DP 12, 2-D DP 11, Tries 3). The fix: add Subsets II to Backtracking, remove the two duplicates, keep progress (it is keyed by link); the owner re-seeds the sheets. The generator also removes duplicates by link, whatever the data.
**Fifth round, 13 Nov 2026 (readiness review of version 7; my fixes, applied under the owner's "whatever is good for PrepStack")**
37. **The day budget, stated once.** Of a day's stated hours: **70 percent for new items, up to 15 percent for due reviews, 15 percent free.** A day with no reviews due keeps the review time free; the Today screen then offers a button "Pull tomorrow's first item forward" (the student chooses, nothing moves by itself). This changes the worked example: new-item capacity is 70 percent of 20 hours a week, **168 hours in 12 weeks against a 203.8-hour syllabus, so about 36 hours are left out** (core first, then the tail), about 92 hours in 8 weeks and about 120 hours in 6 weeks (approximate, to be fixed by the acceptance test once the generator runs).
38. **A mock block is defined.** 90 minutes, two unsolved problems of mixed difficulty from the chosen sheet's remaining core list, timed (a countdown on the screen; nothing is saved), one per week on Saturday from week 4 when Saturday has at least 2 hours; a dated item that is never moved or dropped. If no unsolved core problems remain, no mock is planned.
39. **Refs for every kind of item.** A dsa item is the problem link with its sheet and topic; a notes item is `notes:<subject>:<section>`; a behavioral question is `behavioral:<id>` (the 50-question bank, planned in the last two weeks); a roadmap project is `project:<roadmap>:<step>`; a mock is `mock:<week>`. `refLink` and its titles learn the `behavioral`, `project` and `mock` kinds in Phase 0. Merged report tasks use these minutes: a dsa topic ref 60, a notes ref its estimate, a roadmap ref 120, no ref 30.
40. **Order of CS subjects.** The student picks the subjects; the default order is OS, DBMS, Networks, OOP, and the HLD and LLD notes after them once published. Within the default style, one notes section follows every two DSA topics until the notes run out.
41. **Removed in version 9 (no timer, so no time totals to check).**
42. **Review items are not moved by a rebuild** (they follow their own due days and the cap); a rebuild only re-fits unfinished planned items; the generator reserves the review share of each day at generation (decision 37), and a review that does not fit waits for the next day, oldest and failed first.
**Not in version 1 (decided):** automatic solved detection from LeetCode or Codeforces, leaderboards and contests, coins, FSRS or any per-item learned review model, SM-2 ease factors, time tracking and per-problem time estimates from real times, reminders, email, calendar export, sharing a plan with other people, several plans, AI-written plans, quizzes (B7).

## How the plan is made (by code; one pure module)
1. **Pick the content** for the goal (table above); each item has a ref that already resolves to a real page. Problems already solved on the sheet, topics the student marked as known, and duplicates (the same problem link in two topics) are left out (decision 32).
2. **Order it** by the dependency order in `planCatalog.json`; CS notes follow the order of their `next` chain; the style decides the mixing (default: one topic at a time, with CS notes between DSA topics; the other: mix from the start).
3. **Estimate minutes** (decision 29; adjustable by the student, shown as estimates): a DSA problem to solve and check by difficulty (Basic 30, Easy 40, Medium 70, Hard 120); a notes section = 3 times its reading time plus 5 minutes for the quiz; a roadmap step = the middle of its range split over its topics; a mock block 90 minutes (decision 38); a behavioral question 10 minutes; a review item 3 minutes (a failed problem 20 minutes to re-code).
4. **Fit into days** from the start date to the deadline in IST. Each day gets **85 percent of its hours** (weekday or weekend value; the rest day is empty), split as in decision 37: new items fill up to 70 percent, reviews up to 15 percent. Items are small (a problem, a notes section), never cut mid-way; an item longer than a day is split ("part 1 of 2"); a day is filled as close to its 85 percent as whole items allow, using a smaller item of the same topic to fill a gap (decision 30). When the content ends before the deadline, the spare time is filled by the second pass (decision 31).
5. **Weeks.** The plan is shown in weeks (Monday to Sunday, IST). Each day also holds its **due reviews** (decisions 21 and 22), not a fixed revision block.
6. **When time is short:** keep dated items (a behavioral week, mock blocks) first, then core; drop **extra** topics first, then the tail of the core; list what did not fit ("could not fit") with a link to add it later; if even the core does not fit, say so and suggest the Blind 75 list.
7. **Preview, then change.** Rebuild from today and a change of hours both run the generator on the remaining items and return a **diff** (new finish date, days changed, items moved, done items untouched) without saving; a confirm saves it. Done items and recorded time are never changed.
8. **Merge from a report.** Each report task becomes an item (`source: analyzer:<reportId>`, minutes from its ref type, or 30 without a ref) placed in the free time of the existing plan within the 85 percent hours; when no day has room it goes in the day with the least load and the plan says "over by N minutes". "Replace" starts a new plan from the report's own days. The preview of step 7 is shown first.
9. **On track.** Planned minutes up to today against done minutes, as "On track" or "N days behind" (days = behind minutes divided by the day's minutes, rounded down). At 2 or more days behind, or 3 or more missed items, the plan suggests a rebuild (decision 24). Missed items are shown at the top of today as "carried over" without changing their dates.
10. **Results and reviews.** Ticking an item records the result (decision 20); the review ladder (decision 21) creates **review items** (`type: review`, a ref to the original item and a stage) on their due days, capped at 15 percent of the day's hours (decisions 22 and 37); a notes review is the section's quiz (3 minutes); a rebuild does not move review items (decision 42).
11. **Skip, move and locks.** Skip removes the item from the days and puts it on the skipped list (with the prerequisite warning); Move changes its day by at most 3 days and locks it; a locked item stays where the student put it during any rebuild (decisions 23 and 26).
12. **Lighter days.** A date range with its own hours replaces the weekday hours for those days only; the rest of the plan is re-fitted with the preview (decision 25).
13. **Start small.** In each day the shortest item is first, and the Start button opens it (decision 27).

## Data model
`Plan { user (ref User, unique), goalType (placements | role | bigtech), style (one-topic | mixed), title, status (active | finished), targetRole, sheetSlug, subjects[], knownTopics[], startDate, deadline, hoursByWeekday[7], restDay, items[], coverage { plannedMinutes, syllabusMinutes, leftOut[] }, createdAt, updatedAt }`; each `item { _id, type (dsa | notes | roadmap | review | mock | behavioral | custom), ref, title, plannedMinutes, day (date, IST), part, source (planner | analyzer:<reportId>), starred, locked, skipped, result (alone | hints | solution | null), reviewStage, nextReviewDay, doneAt }`; the plan also holds `lighterDays: [{ from, to, hours }]`. One active plan per user (a unique index on `user` where `status` is `active`; finished plans are kept as history); caps: at most 600 items, title 120 characters, custom item 200. The weekly review is computed from the items when opened, so no extra collection. The plan is deleted with the account (`ref: 'User'` and the existing delete flow); the privacy page and the delete-account card get one line.

## API (login except the preview; zod validators; limiters in `limits.js`)
- **Public:** `POST /api/plan/preview` (the form; returns the coverage numbers and **week 1 only** (the server never sends the other weeks to a logged-out request); no database write; its own limiter per IP, for example `PLAN_PREVIEWS_PER_MINUTE`; inputs capped).
- **Plan (login):** `GET /api/plan` (plan, today's items with due reviews and carried-over items, counts, on-track, the rebuild suggestion), `POST /api/plan` (generate and save; refuses when one exists unless `replace: true`), `DELETE /api/plan` (free, always allowed).
- **Changes with a preview:** `POST /api/plan/changes/preview` (rebuild, new hours, lighter days or add-from-report, returns the diff), `POST /api/plan/changes/apply` (the same body plus the diff's checksum, so only a previewed change can be applied).
- **Items:** `PATCH /api/plan/items/:id` (tick, move day, minutes, remove), `POST /api/plan/items` (add a custom or a content item), `PATCH /api/plan/items/:id/star`, `POST /api/plan/items/:id/result` (alone, hints or solution, or a quiz score for a notes item; sets the review ladder), `POST /api/plan/items/:id/skip` (returns the prerequisite warning when later topics depend on it), `POST /api/plan/items/:id/review` (passed or failed; moves the stage).
- **Review:** `GET /api/plan/review?weekStart=` (planned minutes against the minutes of done items, done, moved, weak topics with their notes and quiz links).
- Ownership filter on every query. Ticking a DSA item also upserts the sheet progress `{ link, solvedAt }` in the same operation (an untick removes it).

## The first screen: one calm "Today" (decision 33, sacred)
**The rule:** the Today screen shows only three things: (1) what the student has to do today, (2) the time needed, (3) one big **Start** button. Everything else is one tap away in the tabs.

**On the screen, above the fold, and nothing else:**
- One line: "Today: 3 items, 2 h 10 min" (the sum of the planned minutes of today's items).
- Today's items as a short list with their minutes: **carried-over** items first, then the day's planned items, then the **due review items** as short lines (a review item is work for today, so it is in the list and in the time; the review ladder and its numbers are not).
- One big **Start** button that opens the first item (the smallest item first).
- When nothing is planned today: the next day that has items, labelled with its date.

**Not on this screen (they live in the tabs):** charts and the progress ring, the "week 3 of 12" bar, the on-track line, the review overview, the weekly review, the revision list, the skipped list, the settings, rebuild and change-my-hours, and long text. A student who is behind sees a small badge on the plan tab ("2 days behind: rebuild?"), not a banner on Today. Every word is plain; a student who has never seen the page knows what to press.

**How this is kept true:** the Today screen is one small component with a fixed list of parts, a client test that renders it and fails on anything extra (a chart, a menu, a second button), and a browser check at 375 px that the Start button is visible without scrolling.

## Tests
- **Generator (pure):** too little time (core first, then tail, coverage line, Blind 75 suggestion), empty sheet, zero study days (rejected), an item longer than a day (split), a rest day, weekday against weekend hours, the 85 percent rule (no day above it), start date after midnight IST, deadline in 1 and 52 weeks, rebuild after a missed week (done items and time untouched), rebuild past the deadline, the diff (new finish date, moved count, protected done items), merge from a report into a full plan, stable ordering and determinism, both study styles, the catalog covers every topic of every sheet, on-track maths; the review ladder (each result gives its gaps, a pass moves on, a fail goes back, a retired item stops), the 15 percent review cap, failed and starred first, overflow stays due and is not deleted, no new items while the backlog is over one day's cap, skip then rebuild keeps the skipped list, move limit (3 days, not onto a mock day), locked items survive a rebuild, lighter days (a range with fewer hours, the return to normal), carried-over items do not change dates, the rebuild suggestion thresholds (2 days or 3 items), the overflow order (dated, core, extra), the smallest item first.
- **API (supertest, in-memory Mongo):** the preview works without login, returns only the numbers and week 1 (the other weeks are not in the response), writes nothing, is limited, and rejects out-of-range input; every other plan route refuses a request without login (401); ownership (another user's plan gives 404); caps; the unique plan; `replace`; apply refuses a change that was not previewed; DSA tick and untick sync with sheet progress (idempotent, streak counted once); star; result and review calls (ownership, valid values, stage changes); skip; review numbers; free delete; delete cascade on account deletion; validators; limiter (429).
- **Acceptance example (decision 35):** the 12-week, 8-week and 6-week cases on the real sheet and notes data, with the corrected estimates and the 70 percent new-item share, asserting the syllabus hours, the weekly hours, the coverage, what was left out (about 36 hours in the 12-week case) and the finish date; also: a problem in two topics is scheduled once; already solved problems and known topics are left out; no day ends with a gap when a smaller item of the topic fits; spare time becomes the second pass and the early-finish message; plan status and the one-active-plan index.
- **Mutation checks** on the ownership filter, the sheet-progress sync, the 85 percent rule, the apply-checksum, the review cap and the lock on moved items: each must make a test fail.
- **Client (Vitest):** week grouping, counts, today's items, coverage and on-track text. **SEO:** the new page is in the prerender and `seo-check`. **Browser (isolated stack):** the builder without login, the login step and the settings carrying through, each goal, tick a DSA item and see it on the sheet, rebuild with the preview, add from a report, the weekly review, delete, 375 px.

## Risks and honesty
- The public preview can be abused: a limiter per IP, capped inputs, no database write, no user data in it.
- Review workload: the ladder and the 15 percent cap exist because six reviews per problem at five new problems a day is about 7 to 17 hours a week; the results are self-reported, so they are a guide and not proof; the gaps are our design and are adjusted after the student check.
- Minutes are estimates, shown as estimates; the plan never promises a result and says what it left out.
- There is no time tracking in version 1 (owner decision): minutes are planned estimates, shown as estimates; if students ask for a timer after the student check it can be added then.
- A plan that falls behind makes people stop: on-track, the preview-and-rebuild, the weekly review and the review ladder exist for that; nothing is deleted without a confirm, and done work is never moved.
- The core and extra labels are our judgement from the Blind 75 list, not a count of what interviews ask; the plan and the notes say so and make no frequency claim.
- Sheets, notes and roadmaps change: a plan keeps the refs and titles it was made with, and a ref that no longer exists is shown as removed, never as a broken link.
- Two sources of ticks (sheet and plan) can drift if a write fails halfway: one operation, with a test that fails it midway.
